Everything you need to go from a reference head or a set of photographs to an editable ASCII portrait.

Your first portrait

You can start sculpting immediately. The reference model is already loaded in the studio, and every edit is rendered in ASCII.

  1. Open the studio and drag the preview to inspect the head.
  2. Choose a control group, then select a point on the model or from the list.
  3. Drag the point to reshape that region. Use Undo and Redo to compare your edits.
  4. Choose Export .html to save a rotatable model, or Export .txt to save the current view.

Edits live in the current tab. Export a model before closing or reloading the editor.

Choose useful photographs

Start with a clear front photograph. A profile and a three-quarter photograph give the fit more information about depth, hair, and the back of the head. Up to eight views are supported.

Keep the photographs consistent

  • Use the same person, hairstyle, and expression in every photograph.
  • Include the whole head, visible ears, and neck down to the collar.
  • Use a simple background and even lighting. Avoid strong filters and hard shadows across the face.
  • Use JPG, PNG, or WebP. If your browser cannot open a phone's HEIC image, export it as JPG first.

Match each image to its angle

The angle labels describe the direction the nose points in the image. Use the studies below as an orientation reference.

These illustrations show the reference scan, not photographs of a fitted subject. The same orientation labels appear in the photo dialog.

Detect once. Apply together.

  1. In the studio, choose Fit from photos.
  2. Load the front photo into 0° · Front. Add any other photographs to their matching slots.
  3. Drag inside each frame to pan. Scroll over a frame to zoom, and use its Rotate slider to level the face. Every slot has its own alignment controls.
  4. Choose Detect landmarks. The progress bar follows the analysis of the loaded images.
  5. Review the detected outlines, then choose Apply fit. All loaded views contribute to the same model.

Use Remove photo to clear a slot. After replacing, removing, or realigning a photograph, detect again before applying the new fit.

Understand estimated views

Estimate features from missing angles uses the front view, symmetry, and the reference head to complete unobserved regions. A front photograph provides proportions and a starting estimate; it does not reveal the exact profile or back of the head.

For a useful first fit, try a front view, one profile, and one three-quarter view. Inspect the result from the opposite side before exporting.

What stays on your device?

The photographs and fitting calculations stay in your browser. The studio downloads MediaPipe and ONNX Runtime components from their external hosts when photo detection starts, and loads the bundled face-parser weights as needed. The first detection may take longer while those files load.

Shape one region at a time

The studio has 128 points arranged into eight groups. Choosing a group shows only its points, so a small feature does not get buried beneath the rest of the controls.

GroupWhat you can refine
Face shapeCheekbones, the jaw, chin, and the face outline
Eyes & browsEye corners, eyelids, brow arches, and brow ends
NoseBridge, tip, nostril wings, and local projection
MouthLip shape and the surrounding mouth region
EarsVisible ear shape and placement
Head & hairHead envelope, crown, temples, and fitted hair volume
NeckFront, sides, back, and the transition beneath the jaw
ShouldersThe shoulder silhouette and base of the bust

Selecting a point from the list turns the model toward it. Dashed points sit beneath hair. Drag the point for a broad change; use the arrow keys for finer adjustments.

Face width and Head width are separate sliders. Use them for overall proportions, then return to individual points for local changes.

Reset shape opens a confirmation dialog. Choose Keep changes to continue editing. Reset is intended for a deliberate fresh start.

Check the silhouette from every side

Drag an empty part of the preview to rotate the model. Scroll over it to zoom, or use the zoom buttons. The view buttons jump to Front, Profile, 3/4, Back, and Top.

Inspect the jaw and chin in profile, compare both three-quarter views, and look behind the ears and at the nape. A feature that looks right from the front may still need an adjustment in depth.

Use Background to choose a comfortable contrast. Changing the background does not repaint the model.

Save the right kind of result

Text: a single view

Export .txt saves the current frame as plain characters. Set the camera and zoom first. Text does not preserve per-character colors or interactive rotation.

HTML: the complete viewer

Export .html saves the mesh and a small viewer in one file. The recipient can rotate and zoom the model without installing the editor or downloading the detection models. This is a viewer, not an editable project file.

Upload the exported HTML beside your own website and embed it using an iframe:

<iframe
  src="portrait.html"
  title="Interactive ASCII portrait"
  width="640"
  height="480"
  style="max-width:100%;border:0"
></iframe>

Replace portrait.html with the name of your exported file. Keep the reference-scan attribution when sharing work derived from the base model.

When a fit needs another pass

“No face detected”

Use a closer, unobstructed front portrait. Keep the forehead and chin inside the frame, level the eyes with Rotate, and detect again.

The side view looks unlike the subject

Check that the image is in the correct slot and that the nose points in the stated direction. Add a real profile if the shape is currently inferred from a front photograph.

Hair, ears, or the neck need refinement

Include those areas in the source frames. Check both sides of the fit and use the matching control group for a local correction. Additional side or rear photos give those regions more observed detail.

The detector cannot load

Check your connection and whether a network filter blocks the external model hosts. Keep the browser tab open until the progress bar finishes. Manual editing of the reference head remains available.

The page was reloaded

The current editing session is not an automatic project backup. If you saved an HTML export, you still have the rotatable model; to resume sculpting, begin a new fit in the editor.
